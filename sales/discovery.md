# Discovery questions

Goal: find out whether the pain is real and priced above EUR 39. You are not closing in
this call. You are qualifying. Twenty minutes is too long; aim for twelve.

## Opening

> I am not selling software. I read tender documents for a living and I want to know how
> it actually works on your side. Can I ask about your last tender?

The point of the opening is to get a story, not a pitch. People answer questions, they
deflect pitches.

## The questions that matter

**1. Walk me through the last tender you bid on.**
Let them talk for three minutes uninterrupted. You are listening for: how long, who
involved, what broke.

**2. How long did it take to read the document, and who did it?**
If the answer is "one person, a few hours", the deal is weak.
If it is "three people over a week", you have a deal.

**3. What happens when a tender is rejected on a technicality? Do you find out why?**
Usually the answer is no. That silence is the entire product. Probe it:
> So you find out after the contract is awarded?
> How often in the last two years have you been rejected on paperwork rather than price?

**4. What is the contract value?**
You need this to price against. Below EUR 10k, disqualify the deal honestly.
Above EUR 100k, EUR 49 is rounding error.

**5. How many tenders do you bid per year?**
One per year means a EUR 49 one-off is the entire relationship. Five per year means the
subscription wedge exists later.

**6. If I sent you back the list of things that disqualify, with page numbers, what would
you do with it?**
The most important question. A real answer is "we would not bid". A fake answer is
"that would be useful".

**7. Who else at your competitors does this?**
Nobody. That is your wedge.

## Closing

Two ways to close, use whichever fits:

**Low friction**
> Send me the PDF of anything live right now. I will send back the deadline and the top
> five disqualifying clauses, free, within 24 hours. If that is not useful, you lose
> nothing.

**Direct**
> The full review is EUR 49 and comes back within 24 hours. For a contract of your size
> that is the cheapest insurance in the business. Send the document and I will start now.

Do not follow up with a third option. Pick one.

## Signals to stop

Stop and disqualify politely if:
- they have never lost a tender on paperwork,
- the contract value is small,
- "we have a consultant who does this",
- they want a feature list instead of the answer.

These are not bad people. They are not buyers. Moving on is not rudeness, it is the only
way to reach EUR 1k MRR in a year with five hours a week.

## After every call

Write three lines, same day:
1. What did they lose last time?
2. What did they say yes to?
3. When do I follow up, and on what?

Twenty-five words. If you cannot fill three lines, the call taught you nothing and you
were talking to the wrong person.

## The only question that matters if you have no time

> "Last time you lost a tender you thought you would win, what actually happened?"

Their answer is your entire business. Write it down verbatim and reuse it in every
message.
